What a clean copy covers

One document. When you open a file in the text editor your browser works out a fingerprint of it, and that fingerprint is what the payment is recorded against — the file itself is never sent to us, which is the whole point of that editor.

  • It follows the original file, not one particular set of edits. Open the same file again on the same browser next week, edit it differently, and saving a clean copy is still unlocked. You are not paying per save.

  • It does not expire.

How your purchase is remembered — read this before paying

There are no accounts here, which is deliberate: it means we hold no name, no email address and no password for you. The price of that is worth understanding before you spend anything.

When you pay, this server issues your browser an anonymous token and your browser keeps it in its own storage for this site. Your credits, and any document you have unlocked, belong to that token.

  • Clearing this site's data loses it — including "clear cookies and site data" in your browser settings, and some privacy cleaners.
  • It does not travel. Another browser, another device, or a private window will not have it.
  • Whoever holds the token holds the credits. It works like a cinema ticket rather than an account: there is nothing to log in to, and no way for us to tell that a lost one was yours.
  • Keep your PayPal receipt. It is the only record of the purchase that is yours rather than ours, and it carries the reference we would need to put something right.

Your right to cancel, and why paying here gives it up

For most things bought online a consumer has 14 days to cancel. Digital content supplied immediately is treated differently: under the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013, that right is lost once supply begins, if you have asked for it to begin straight away and understood that you were giving the right up.

So, plainly: when you press the pay button you are asking us to supply the clean copy, or the credits, immediately. Once that has happened you will not have a 14-day right to change your mind. This is said next to the button as well as here, so that nobody meets it for the first time afterwards.

That is about the statutory cooling-off period only. It is not a "no refunds" rule, and it takes nothing away from the rest of this page or from your statutory rights below.
